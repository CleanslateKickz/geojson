# Property Buyout Strategy Analysis

## Property Overview
- **Clackamas**: $700,000 value, $42,000 income (6% cap rate)
- **Tigard**: $1,547,692 value, $114,000 income (6.5% cap rate)
- **Current Ownership**: She owns 40%, ex-husband owns 60% of both properties
- **Goal**: She acquires 100% of Tigard, exits Clackamas entirely

## Financial Requirements
- **Her current Tigard equity**: $619,077 (40% × $1,547,692)
- **His Tigard equity to acquire**: $928,615 (60% × $1,547,692)
- **Her Clackamas equity**: $280,000 (40% × $700,000)
- **Net cash needed**: $648,615 ($928,615 - $280,000)

## Strategy Options Ranked by Viability

### 🥇 **Option 1: Refinance + Partial Asset Swap (Hybrid Approach)**
**Structure**: Combine refinancing with strategic asset reallocation

**Steps**:
1. Refinance Tigard at 75% LTV = $1,160,769 loan
2. Current loan payoff (estimate ~$600,000) leaves ~$560,000 cash
3. She trades her $280,000 Clackamas equity for additional Tigard equity
4. Use refinance proceeds to buy out remaining portion

**Advantages**:
- No capital gains tax on the swap portion
- Immediate liquidity from refinancing
- Clean exit strategy
- Maintains 1031 exchange potential for future

**Risks**:
- Debt service coverage (DSC) analysis needed
- Requires ex-husband's cooperation

---

### 🥈 **Option 2: Modified 1031 Exchange with TIC Restructure**
**Structure**: Convert to TIC, establish holding period, then execute strategic exchange

**Timeline**:
- **Months 1-2**: Restructure LLC to TIC format
- **Months 6-12**: Hold period to establish legitimate TIC ownership
- **Month 13+**: Execute 1031 exchange

**Exchange Strategy**:
1. She sells her Clackamas TIC interest ($280,000)
2. Simultaneously, he sells portion of his Tigard TIC interest to third party
3. She uses 1031 exchange to acquire that third-party interest in Tigard
4. Separate negotiation for remaining buyout

**Advantages**:
- Tax-deferred growth
- Avoids related-party restrictions
- Creates market-based valuation

**Risks**:
- Complex coordination required
- Step-transaction doctrine concerns
- 12+ month timeline

---

### 🥉 **Option 3: Installment Sale with Cross-Collateralization**
**Structure**: Structured buyout using both properties as security

**Mechanics**:
1. She gives him her Clackamas interest ($280,000 credit)
2. He seller-finances remaining $648,615 over 7-10 years
3. Both properties secure the note initially
4. Clackamas released from lien once his equity threshold met

**Payment Structure**:
- Interest rate: 5-6% (market competitive)
- Annual payment: ~$75,000-85,000
- Tigard cash flow ($114K) covers payments comfortably

**Advantages**:
- Preserves his tax deferral (installment sale treatment)
- No immediate financing constraints on her
- Flexible terms negotiation

---

### **Option 4: Delaware Statutory Trust (DST) Conversion**
**Structure**: Convert properties to DST interests, then facilitate exchanges

**Process**:
1. Contribute both properties to DST
2. Issue beneficial interests proportional to ownership
3. She exchanges her Clackamas DST interest for additional Tigard DST interest
4. Eventually, DST can be dissolved with her owning 100% Tigard

**Advantages**:
- Professional management structure
- 1031 exchange eligible
- Cleanly separates ownership interests

**Considerations**:
- Setup costs ($15,000-25,000)
- Ongoing management fees
- SEC compliance requirements

## Risk Mitigation Factors

### **Related Party Exchange Concerns**
- **Safe Harbor**: Use third-party intermediary for any exchanges
- **Timing**: Maintain 12+ month holding periods
- **Substance**: Ensure legitimate business purposes beyond tax avoidance

### **Step Transaction Doctrine**
- **Documentation**: Separate agreements for each transaction phase
- **Business Purpose**: Establish independent business reasons
- **Timing**: Avoid simultaneous or interdependent transactions

### **Debt Service Coverage Analysis**
- **Current Income**: $114,000 Tigard
- **Maximum Debt Service**: ~$85,000 (1.35x DSC ratio)
- **Loan Capacity**: ~$1.1M at 7.5%, 25-year amortization

## Recommended Action Plan

### **Phase 1: Due Diligence (30 days)**
1. Obtain professional appraisals for both properties
2. Analyze current loan terms and prepayment penalties
3. Engage qualified intermediary for 1031 consultation
4. Consult tax attorney on related-party implications

### **Phase 2: Negotiation (30-45 days)**
1. Present multiple options to ex-husband
2. Negotiate preferred structure and timeline  
3. Draft letter of intent with key terms
4. Engage legal counsel for documentation

### **Phase 3: Execution (60-90 days)**
1. Complete chosen transaction structure
2. Handle all required regulatory filings
3. Transfer titles and update insurance
4. Establish new property management if needed

## Professional Team Requirements
- **Real Estate Attorney**: Entity restructuring, 1031 compliance
- **Tax CPA**: Capital gains optimization, related-party rules
- **Qualified Intermediary**: 1031 exchange facilitation  
- **Commercial Lender**: Refinancing analysis and execution
- **Appraiser**: Current market valuations

## Bottom Line Recommendation
**Hybrid Approach (Option 1)** offers the best combination of speed, tax efficiency, and financial feasibility. The refinancing provides immediate liquidity while the asset swap portion avoids capital gains on a significant portion of the transaction. This strategy can typically be executed within 90 days and provides the cleanest long-term ownership structure.
