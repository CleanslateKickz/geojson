Walmart  is testing dark stores, or brick and mortar locations that fulfill online orders but are not open to the public. The Company is currently piloting the concept in Dallas, TX, and another is planned for Bentonville, AR, the Company's hometown. Dark stores will carry some of the Company's most popular products. 

In other news, Walmart and Target are running sales events to compete with Amazon's Prime Day sale, which takes place between July 8 and 11. Walmart and Target are cutting prices on select home electronics, back-to-school supplies, and more.

United Natural Foods provided an update regarding the unauthorized activity on certain information technology systems on June 5, 2025. As previously disclosed, the Company promptly activated its incident response plan and implemented containment measures, including proactively taking certain systems offline,   which temporarily impacted the Company’s ability to fulfill and distribute customer orders.   At this time, the Company does not anticipate sending any notifications to individual consumers as a result of this incident because it did not involve a breach of security of personal information or protected health information.

The unauthorized activity has since been contained and the Company is now regularly receiving and shipping products to retailers across its distribution network. Importantly, the Company has safely restored the core systems that its customers and suppliers use for electronic ordering and invoicing, which has enabled business operations to normalize. Throughout this process, the Company focused on proactively and transparently engaging with its customers and suppliers to support their unique business needs.

In our June 30 Alert , Big 5 Sporting Goods entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Worldwide Sports Group Holdings LLC. A buyer group led by specialty golf operator Worldwide Golf and investment firm Capitol Hill Group will acquire the chain through their newly formed affiliate, WSG Merger LLC. Under the terms of the agreement, Big 5 shareholders will receive $1.45 per share in cash, representing a 36% premium to the Company’s 60-day volume-weighted average price. Including the assumption of approximately $71 million in revolver borrowings, the transaction implies a total enterprise value of roughly $113 million. Until the deal is completed, Big 5 is expected to operate in the ordinary course of business, although the board retains the right to consider a bona fide superior proposal if required by its fiduciary duties.

The transaction is subject to customary closing conditions, including approval by Big 5’s shareholders, the absence of legal or regulatory impediments, no material adverse changes to the business and inventory levels meeting a negotiated threshold. Either party may terminate the agreement if the deal is not completed by November 26, 2025. In the event the Company accepts a superior offer, Big 5 would be required to pay a $2 million termination fee. Conversely, if the buyer group fails to close as required under the agreement, they would owe Big 5 a $3 million reverse termination fee. The Company’s board of directors has unanimously approved the transaction, deeming it fair and in the best interests of shareholders.

In our June 30 Alert , we reported that The Home Depot entered into an agreement for its specialty trade distribution subsidiary, SRS Distribution Inc. (SRS), to acquire GMS Inc. (GMS), a leading North American specialty building products distributor. The transaction is expected to further the Company's strategy of targeting professional contractors. GMS provides   building products, such as drywall, ceilings and other items.

Under the terms of the agreement, a subsidiary of SRS will commence a cash tender offer to purchase all outstanding shares of GMS common stock for $110 per share, reflecting a total equity value of approximately $4.30 billion and implying a total enterprise value (including net debt) of approximately $5.50 billion. The completion of the tender offer is subject to customary closing conditions, including the receipt of required regulatory approvals and the tender of a number of shares of GMS common stock representing a majority of the outstanding shares, and is expected to be completed by the end of FY25. The transaction is expected to be funded through cash on hand and debt and is not expected to alter the Company's plan to return to a 2.0x leverage ratio by the end of FY26. The Company expects the deal to be accretive to adjusted EPS in the first year following closing, excluding synergies.

As indicated in our June 27 Alert , Chipotle Mexican Grill entered into a new senior unsecured Revolving Credit Agreement with JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. The agreement provides for a $500 million revolving credit facility that matures on June 24, 2030, and includes a $20 million sub-facility for letters of credit. Borrowings bear interest at term SOFR plus a margin of 1.125% to 1.875% or an alternate base rate plus a margin of 0.125% to 0.875%, with the specific margin in either case based on the Company's total leverage ratio. The facility includes several financial covenants, including a maximum total leverage ratio of 3x, a minimum consolidated fixed charge coverage ratio of 1.5x, and limitations on the Company's ability to create liens on its property, incur debt, pay dividends, and merge with other companies.

In connection with entering into the new Revolving Credit Agreement, the Company terminated its previous credit agreement, which included a $500 million revolver that would have matured on April 13, 2026. There were no borrowings outstanding under the revolver at the time of its termination.
